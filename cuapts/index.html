Context

What is CUApts

A tool that helps Cornell students navigate off-campus housing by browsing listings, comparing options, and reading real apartment reviews. It brings housing information into one place so students can make more informed decisions about where to live in Ithaca.

In Fall 2025, CUApts aims to move beyond housing discovery toward a more holistic housing planning experience. I designed a Saved Folders system that allows students to organize apartments, add notes, and share folders with roommates, supporting more personalized and collaborative decision-making.

Problem

Students need more than saved listings

While CUApts makes it easy to discover and bookmark apartments, it offers limited support once students start comparing options and coordinating with roommates. As a result, the housing search process lacks:

Organization

Saved apartments live in a single flat list, making it hard to categorize, prioritize, or track progress.

Collaboration

Students can’t share saved apartments, leave notes, or coordinate decisions with roommates in one place.

Planning

Bookmarks lack tools for comparison, pros and cons, or decision status, pushing users to external tools.

User Research

Students struggle to organize saved apartments

From my research, I found that students struggle to organize and compare apartment options over time. Many rely on external tools to manage their search and coordinate with roommates.

Key Insights

Housing decisions need shared planning

Based on an evaluation of CUApts’ current features and common student housing behaviors, these insights highlight key gaps in how users organize, plan, and decide on housing together.

Housing is a shared decision

Students rarely choose apartments alone and need tools that support coordination with roommates.

Decisions unfold over time

Students revisit listings repeatedly and need ways to compare, update, and track options across weeks.

Students need better tools

When planning tools are missing, students rely on external apps like notes or group chats.

Key Design Decisions

Save to folder

To support the new Saved Folders feature, I explored different interaction patterns for saving apartments. The goal was to allow users to save listings quickly while minimizing confusion and accidental actions during browsing.

Checkbox exploration

Supports saving to multiple folders at once

Less familiar pattern for housing apps

Higher risk of accidental selection during browsing

Saved button (final)

Provides clear status feedback (saved vs. unsaved)

Matches familiar save interactions across housing and shopping apps

Reduces accidental actions during fast scrolling

I chose the Save button interaction because it balanced clarity, familiarity, and error prevention, which were more critical than multi-folder saving at the moment of discovery.

Save to new folder

Clicking save to new folder

Keeps users in context without leaving the current view

UI change feels unexpected and visually disconnected from the action

Inline text input increases risk of hesitation or misclicks during saving

Create folder modal (final)

Uses a familiar modal pattern that clearly communicates a new step

Provides focused attention on naming the folder

Creates a predictable flow that feels intentional and easy to follow

Reflection

What did I learn?

This was my first semester on the DTI team, and it significantly strengthened my foundation in design principles and critique. Through regular design reviews, I learned how to clearly articulate design decisions, receive constructive feedback, and iterate more intentionally based on critique rather than personal preference.

I also gained valuable experience collaborating cross-functionally with PMs and developers. Working closely with them helped me understand design feasibility, tradeoffs, and how to balance ideal user experiences with technical and timeline constraints, ultimately shaping more realistic and implementable designs.
